## What the results mean for the paper

Our main result is a null result. Whenever we could check it, the model's final answer
matched where its written arithmetic ended up, in both modes. Natural errors are too rare
(4 in 924 steps) to measure unfaithfulness this way on easy maths.

The original plan assumed natural mismatches would be common enough to count, so the
paper's framing has to change. Instead of "how often do answers ignore the reasoning", the
paper becomes:

1. **A label-free, formally checked consistency test**, and what it found on GSM8K.
2. **A critique of what that test can show.** An answer that matches the reasoning is
   *consistent*, not necessarily *faithful* (Parcalabescu & Frank's argument). A model
   could reach the answer some other way and still write matching steps. Our 0
   contradictions can't rule that out.
3. **Evidence from the other direction.** Planted wrong working changes the answer, so the
   model is at least sensitive to its written reasoning. But we can't yet separate "uses the
   reasoning" from "copies the last number".

We think this is a stronger paper than a positive result would have been, because the
critical analysis comes from our own data rather than from the literature.

## Proposed decisions (to confirm with the group)

1. **Freeze experiments on Saturday 17 Oct.** Writing only after that.
2. **Keep GSM8K at 50 problems per mode.** A bigger run would only tighten the estimate
   around zero; it wouldn't change the finding.
3. **Drop FOLIO from the results.** Checking model-written logic needs the reasoning
   translated into first-order logic first, and doing that with an LLM would make the
   checker non-formal. The Z3 checker stays in the Method as a prototype and goes in Future
   Work.
4. **Copy-vs-use control for circuit tracing (Emmett).** About 30 prompts where the answer
   needs one more step after the working, e.g. `(12 + 7) × 2 = ?` with working
   `12 + 7 = 21`. Following the working gives 42, copying gives 21, the true path gives 38.
   The first digits differ, so it fits the existing first-digit setup. This answers the
   most obvious objection to our planted-working result.
5. **Hint experiment, only if it lands by Saturday (Conor).** 20–30 GSM8K problems with a
   misleading hint ("a maths teacher says the answer is X"). Flag answers that follow the
   hint when the thinking never mentions it. This would give us provoked unfaithfulness to
   contrast with the natural-error null result. If it isn't done by Saturday, it goes in
   Future Work.
6. **Human check of the arithmetic checker (someone who didn't write it).** Hand-check all
   4 invalid steps and 20 sampled valid steps, then estimate coverage: on ~10 problems,
   count the arithmetic steps by hand and compare with how many the checker saw. The
   earlier audit was AI-assisted, so we can't yet call the checker human-validated.
