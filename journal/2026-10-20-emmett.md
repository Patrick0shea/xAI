- Wrong working: copying has mean first-digit probability 0.858757 and is the
  global top token in 30/30 cases. Using the sum then multiplying has mean
  probability 0.036832; the true final digit has 0.016416. Neither is top.
- Correct working: the digit of the correct intermediate sum is top in 30/30
  cases (mean probability 0.880756); the final correct digit is never top
  (mean probability 0.046436).
- No working: the true final digit is top in 20/30 (mean probability 0.386672).

The baseline diagnosis matters: the exported summary deliberately keeps the
wrong-condition candidate labels in every condition, so its baseline `copy`
field does NOT mean copying the correct intermediate sum. The separate
`analyze_copy_vs_use.py` script verifies the export and computes the appropriate
intermediate digit per condition. Its `other` counts use those baseline targets
and therefore differ from the exported wrong-hypothesis classification.

## Why

The earlier planted-working examples cannot distinguish arithmetic use from
copying. The extra operation separates their predicted first digits. Correct
working and no-working baselines check whether the model can solve the same
questions without the planted error. Probabilities remain unconditional over
the full vocabulary; they must not be presented as prompt frequencies.

The new runner uses original Hugging Face weights; the earlier circuit
notebook used a transcoder replacement model. That difference limits direct
comparison. Neither setup establishes dependence of the 1.7B model's
self-generated reasoning, and first-digit agreement does not imply a correct
complete answer.
