# First Paper - 24 September

**Paper:** A. Jacovi and Y. Goldberg, "Towards Faithfully Interpretable NLP Systems: How Should We Define and Evaluate Faithfulness?"
**Link**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03685

## Key questions the paper asks

- What is interpretability?
- What makes an interpretation high-quality?

## Core arguments

- **Faithfulness ≠ plausibility.** Faithfulness (does the interpretation reflect
the model's actual reasoning?) should be defined and evaluated explicitly,
and independently of plausibility (does it convince a human?).
- **A plausible interpretation isn't necessarily a faithful one.** An explanation
can sound convincing without reflecting how the model actually reached its decision.
- **Faithfulness should be graded, not binary.** Instead of asking "is this
interpretation faithful: yes/no?", the authors call for graded criteria that
measure the extent and likelihood of an interpretation being faithful in practice.
