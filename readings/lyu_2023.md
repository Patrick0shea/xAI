# Lyu et al. reading notes - 21 October 2026

**Paper:** Qing Lyu et al., "Faithful Chain-of-Thought Reasoning."
**Venue:** IJCNLP-AACL 2023, pp. 305-329.
**Source reviewed:** [Published paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.ijcnlp-main.20.pdf).
**Report citation key:** `lyu2023`.
**Prepared for:** Conor's literature contribution.

## Key question

Can a system make its answer depend on an explicit reasoning chain by construction?

## Core arguments and method

Faithful CoT separates translation from execution. An LM generates natural-language subproblems alongside symbolic instructions; a deterministic solver executes those instructions to obtain the answer. Supported representations include Python, Datalog and PDDL (Section 3; Figure 2).

The authors report that the Codex-based system beats standard CoT on nine of ten datasets spanning arithmetic, planning, multi-hop QA and relational inference (Section 5.1). Removing the solver substantially reduces accuracy on several tasks, although SayCan is an exception (Section 5.2).

The guarantee concerns the executable chain's role in producing the answer. It does not make the LM's translation process interpretable, nor guarantee that the translation or answer is correct (Introduction, footnote 2; Limitations).

## Relevance to our project

Our checker has a different role: Qwen3 generates a response first, and SymPy or Z3 checks selected expressions or formulas afterwards. The solver does not produce Qwen3's answer. Consequently, agreement between a checked derivation and an answer cannot inherit Faithful CoT's execution guarantee. This is our methodological comparison, rather than a result reported by Lyu et al.

For Method C, distinguish whether a formula follows from the premises from whether that formula accurately represents the response. FOLIO label agreement validates part of the checking pipeline; it does not show that Qwen3 used a particular deduction.

## Impact on the paper

- Related Work: contrast generating answers through symbolic execution with verifying an already generated trace.
- Method: describe the checker as an evaluator, and state how natural-language steps become symbolic inputs.
- Limitations: retain the translation boundary and avoid describing a passing trace as a faithful account of internal computation.

These are recommendations for our draft, not additional experiments. A future solver-backed generation baseline would change the answer-producing system and should be evaluated separately.

[Han et al.](han_2024_folio.md) support benchmark-based logic validation; [Lanham et al.](lanham_2023.md) motivate interventions on reasoning. Together, these readings suggest separating formal correctness, answer agreement and evidence of causal dependence in our reporting.
