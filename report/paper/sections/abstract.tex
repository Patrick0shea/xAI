\begin{abstract}
Reasoning models write out their working before answering, and readers may take that
working as an explanation. We test whether Qwen3-1.7B's final answers are consistent
with its own written arithmetic. A label-free checker extracts each calculation,
re-evaluates it with exact rational arithmetic, and compares the final answer with
the answer the working reaches. On 50 paired GSM8K problems, accuracy was 92\% with
thinking and 80\% without, and no final answer contradicted its working in any of
the 83 comparable responses (95\% upper bound 4.3\%). In a copy-vs-use control on
Qwen3-0.6B, the first digit of a planted wrong intermediate result was the top
prediction in all 30 prompts, suggesting the working was copied rather than computed
with. With a misleading hint, \textbf{TBD} of \textbf{TBD} thinking-mode answers
followed it. Passing a consistency check therefore does not establish that written
reasoning is faithful to how the answer was produced.
\end{abstract}
