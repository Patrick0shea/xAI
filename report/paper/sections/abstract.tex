\begin{abstract}
Reasoning models write out their working before answering, and readers may take it as
an explanation. We test whether Qwen3-1.7B's final answers are consistent with its own
written arithmetic, using a label-free checker that re-evaluates each extracted
calculation with exact rational arithmetic and compares the final answer with the
answer the working reaches. On 50 paired GSM8K problems, accuracy was 92\% with
thinking and 80\% without, and no answer contradicted its working in 83 comparable
responses (95\% upper bound 4.3\%). A copy-vs-use control on Qwen3-0.6B suggests that
sensitivity to planted working reflects copying rather than computation: the copied
digit was the top prediction in all 30 prompts. A misleading hint redirected 4 of 50
thinking-mode answers, all of which acknowledged it. Passing a consistency check does
not establish that written reasoning is faithful to how the answer was produced.
\end{abstract}
