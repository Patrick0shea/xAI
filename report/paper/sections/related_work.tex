\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% Shared context and Emmett's hint-literature contribution.
Faithfulness concerns whether an explanation reflects the model's actual
reasoning, distinct from whether it appears plausible~\cite{jacovi2020}.
Turpin et al.~\cite{turpin2023} show that input biases can change answers
while remaining unmentioned in their explanations. Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025}
extend hint-based tests to reasoning models, measuring acknowledgement among
paired responses that switch to the hinted answer. These motivate intervention
controls alongside textual consistency checks. Arcuschin et al.~\cite{arcuschin2025}
also investigate unfaithful reasoning on unmanipulated prompts, so natural-setting
evaluation is not itself a novel contribution of our study.

% Conor: Lanham, NSF-CoT, Lyu and Parcalabescu.
Lanham et al.~\cite{lanham2023} probe answer dependence by truncating,
corrupting and paraphrasing reasoning. NSF-CoT~\cite{pramanik2026} combines
formal verification with an LLM entailment component to assess written steps.
Lyu et al.'s Faithful CoT~\cite{lyu2023} generates symbolic instructions and
executes them with a deterministic solver, making that chain responsible for
the answer without guaranteeing correct translation. Our checker instead
evaluates already generated arithmetic or formal annotations; it does not
produce Qwen3's answer. Parcalabescu and Frank~\cite{parcalabescu2024} distinguish
self-consistency from faithfulness to internal computation; their CC-SHAP
compares input contributions to answer and explanation generation. Our narrower
measure checks agreement between recoverable working and the final answer.
Thus deterministic verification complements interventions but cannot establish
that written reasoning caused an answer.
