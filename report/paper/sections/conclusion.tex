\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
The natural-output evaluation covers one model and the first 50 GSM8K problems,
limiting generalisation. Different sampling settings confound attributing accuracy
gains solely to thinking. The contradiction bound excludes 17 incomparable
responses; paired responses and repeated steps are dependent. Extraction misses
prose and unit-bearing calculations, while valid arithmetic can misinterpret a
problem. Repetition and truncation further restrict the evidence.

FOLIO validates supplied annotations only, with eight unresolved label disagreements;
no model reasoning was checked. Copy-vs-use was run on Qwen3-0.6B only, whereas
the main evaluation uses 1.7B. Constructed prompts and first-digit probabilities
cannot establish population rates or full-answer behaviour. Tracing approximates
the network, and attribution graphs alone do not establish causality. All five
hint followers acknowledged the suggestion, but hardware differences limit
causal attribution to the hint.

Future work should validate extraction coverage, test harder problems, review
logical translations and apply controlled feature interventions. Thinking improved
accuracy in this sample, but zero detected answer--working contradictions
establishes observable consistency, including agreement with faulty working,
rather than faithful internal computation. The copy-vs-use result likewise
cautions against treating prompt sensitivity as arithmetic use.
