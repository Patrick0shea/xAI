\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Our natural-output evaluation uses the first 50 GSM8K test problems and one
model, Qwen3-1.7B. This small, non-random subset limits generalisation, and
different sampling settings prevent attributing the accuracy difference solely
to thinking. The contradiction bound covers only 83 comparable responses,
excluding 17 others. Paired responses and repeated steps are dependent; the
pooled response and step intervals do not fully account for this.

The checker verifies supported numerical equalities, rather than every inference
or the interpretation of the problem. A provisional ten-response review identifies
missed calculations in prose and expressions with units; independent human
confirmation remains outstanding. Repetition loops and one truncated thinking
response further restrict the evidence. FOLIO validates the checker on supplied
annotations only: no model reasoning was translated into logic and checked, and
eight label disagreements remain unresolved.

Planted-working and tracing experiments use Qwen3-0.6B rather than 1.7B.
Eight hand-made addition prompts and first-digit probabilities cannot establish
population rates or full-answer behaviour. Tracing uses a transcoder replacement
model, whereas copy-vs-use uses original weights; attribution graphs alone do not
establish causality. The copy-vs-use control favours repeating the supplied
intermediate value with both wrong and correct working. This limits an
interpretation of prompt sensitivity as arithmetic use, without establishing an
internal copying mechanism. Future work should validate extraction with human
review, test harder problems, translate reasoning into reviewed logical formulas,
and apply controlled feature interventions. Hint results are still required to
assess responses to misleading advice.

Thinking improved accuracy in this sample, but neither mode produced a detected
answer--working contradiction among comparable responses. Agreement is evidence
of observable consistency, including agreement with faulty working; it does not
establish that the written reasoning caused the answer.
