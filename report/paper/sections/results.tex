\subsection{GSM8K: accuracy and answer--reasoning consistency}\label{sec:res-gsm8k}
% OWNER: Fionn. STARTER DRAFT (written with Claude Code, 2026-10-20): Fionn to
% check every sentence against its source, rewrite, and commit as his own.
%    consistency" (Table I is already in place)
% Sources: outputs/clean/paper_stats.json;
% outputs/clean/qwen3_1.7b_gsm8k_50_completed_report.md ("Five examples");
% outputs/clean/qwen3_1.7b_gsm8k_50_completed_arithmetic_checked.jsonl
Thinking mode answered 46 of 50 problems correctly and no-thinking mode 40
(Table~\ref{tab:gsm8k}). All six discordant pairs favour thinking (IDs 5, 8, 11, 12,
43 and 45), with none the other way (exact McNemar $p = 0.031$).

The checker extracted 762 arithmetic steps from the thinking traces and 162 from the
no-thinking responses. Four were wrong: three in thinking (two repeats of one error in
ID~21, and one in ID~26 that the model corrected itself) and one in no-thinking
(ID~11). An implied answer could be extracted for 45 thinking and 38 no-thinking
responses. In every one of these 83, the final answer matched it, so the pooled
contradiction rate is 0 (Clopper--Pearson 95\% upper bound 4.3\%). The other 17
responses had no implied answer, or were truncated (thinking ID~21, which looped until
the token limit).

Two cases show what the check can and cannot see. In ID~26 (thinking), the model wrote
$49.50+67.50+126.00=243.50$, noticed the error and corrected it to 243, and its final
answer was right. In ID~7 (thinking), every step was valid, but the model misread the
problem and answered 120 instead of 160: valid arithmetic cannot catch an
interpretation error.

\subsection{Planted working and copy-vs-use}\label{sec:res-planted}
% OWNER: Emmett. STARTER DRAFT (written with Claude Code, 2026-10-20): Emmett to
% check every sentence against its source, rewrite, and commit as his own.
% Sources: outputs/circuits/hand_made_behavioural.csv, hand_made_baseline.csv
% (Table II already summarises them -- check it); journal/2026-10-07-circuit-tracing.md
With no working, Qwen3-0.6B gives the true answer's first digit with probability
0.68--0.83 (mean 0.75), and with a correct working 0.99 on all 8 prompts
(Table~\ref{tab:planted}). With a wrong working, the first digit follows the working
with probability 0.94--0.99, and the true digit falls to at most 0.013. A wrong working
therefore overrides the model's own default answer. These are 8 hand-picked prompts
on a small model, so they demonstrate the effect rather than measure a rate.

checker's verdict matched the dataset label for 191 of them (Table~\ref{tab:folio}).

premises. The eight disagreements have not yet been audited against the
natural-language text.
