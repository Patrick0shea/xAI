\subsection{GSM8K: accuracy and answer--reasoning consistency}\label{sec:res-gsm8k}
% OWNER: Fionn. OUTLINE:
% - Table I. Thinking is more accurate: 6 problems only thinking solves (IDs 5, 8,

Thinking answered 46/50 problems correctly (92\%), compared with 40/50 (80\%)
without thinking (Table~\ref{tab:gsm8k}). Six problems were correct only with
thinking, none only without it (exact McNemar $p=0.03125$). These are the first
50 test problems, not a random benchmark sample.

The checker detected no final-answer contradictions among 45 thinking and 38
no-thinking responses: 0/83 pooled, with a two-sided exact 95\% interval whose
upper endpoint is 4.3\%. This bound concerns checker-comparable responses
under the interval's sampling assumptions. It excludes 17 responses and does
not establish use of written reasoning. Because responses are paired within
problems, the pooled interval is descriptive and does not account for that
dependence. The regenerated denominator of 83 supersedes the unreproducible
earlier count of 74.

Comparability requires an extracted implied answer and a completed response.
Unsupported expressions and calculations missed by extraction therefore limit
what the absence of detected contradictions can show.

Four of 924 checked arithmetic steps were invalid: three with thinking, one
without. Thinking ID~26 corrected its addition error and answered correctly.
No-thinking ID~11 wrote $204+160+330=700$ and answered 700, although the sum
is 694. Thus agreement with erroneous working passes a consistency test
without establishing correctness. One thinking response exhausted its token
budget. Independent human checker-audit results remain outstanding; these
counts describe extracted arithmetic, rather than all calculations in the
responses.

\subsection{Planted working and copy-vs-use}\label{sec:res-planted}

% OWNER: Emmett. Copy-vs-use numbers: outputs/clean/copy_vs_use_summary.json.
All planted-working and copy-vs-use experiments used Qwen3-0.6B only.
In the eight addition examples (Table~\ref{tab:planted}), wrong working
shifted probability towards its stated answer. However, copying and
arithmetic use predict the same digit in this setup, so that result alone
cannot distinguish them.

The copy-vs-use control separates those predictions. With wrong working,
the model copies the planted number in 30/30 global top-token predictions
(mean probability $\approx0.86$), rather than carrying on from it
($\approx0.04$) or giving the true answer ($\approx0.02$). The latter two
candidates are never the global top token; Table~\ref{tab:copyuse} gives
three-decimal means. Without working, the true-answer digit is top in
20/30 cases (mean probability 0.387). With correct working, the true-answer
digit has mean probability 0.046 and is never top. Candidate labels remain
fixed to the wrong-working hypotheses; the summary's baseline ``copy''
category therefore does not measure copying the correct intermediate sum.

This supports copying as the behavioural description on these constructed
prefixes; it does not establish an internal copying mechanism or complete-answer
accuracy. Figure~\ref{fig:tracing} illustrates the separate tracing setup,
without analysed edges or interventions. Neither these 0.6B results nor the
illustrative graph tells us whether Qwen3-1.7B uses its reasoning.

\subsection{Hint test}\label{sec:res-hint}
% OWNER: Fionn. OUTLINE:
% - Table V: complete answer counts and human disclosure review by Fionn.
% - Revised deadline: Friday 23 Oct, 14:00 Europe/Dublin (22 Oct instruction).
% - Populate from the complete summary only after named human attribution review.
% - Results C target: about 150 words. Cover paired switches, acknowledgement,
%   accuracy, truncation and the runtime confound; no preliminary totals here.
% Source: outputs/clean/qwen3_1.7b_gsm8k_50_hint_summary.json;
% all 100 raw responses present; all five human review verdicts confirmed.
Across 50 problems per mode, four thinking answers (8\%) and one no-thinking
answer (2\%) matched the incorrect hint (Table~\ref{tab:hint}). None already
matched it at baseline. Two thinking followers and the no-thinking follower
had previously answered correctly. Thus all five are new switches, including
three from correct answers.

Thinking accuracy fell from 46/50 to 38/50, while no-thinking accuracy rose
from 40/50 to 41/50. Thinking generated 2.82 times as many tokens on average
with the hint. Eight thinking responses reached the token limit, compared
with one at baseline; six previously correct responses did not finish. Neither
no-thinking condition had truncations.

All five followers acknowledged the supplied answer in their reasoning, as
confirmed by human review; no undisclosed hint following was observed. These
outcomes show prompt sensitivity, but runtime differences from the earlier
baseline limit attribution to the hint alone. Acknowledgement also does not
establish faithful internal computation.
