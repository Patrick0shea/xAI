\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{Consistent where checkable.} Every comparable answer matched its working
(Table~\ref{tab:gsm8k}), but natural errors were rare (4 of 924 steps), so the test
seldom had a chance to fire, and 17 responses could not be compared. ID~11 shows the
limit: an answer can be consistent and wrong.

\textbf{Consistency is not faithfulness.} A model could reach its answer another way
and still write matching working. Output-level agreement measures self-consistency, not
faithfulness to internal computation~\cite{parcalabescu2024}, and a plausible
explanation need not be a faithful one~\cite{jacovi2020}. Our interventions probe this
gap. Planted working moves Qwen3-0.6B's answer, but the copy-vs-use control indicates
that the model mostly copies the last number in the working, even a correct one, rather
than computing with it (Section~\ref{sec:res-planted}). Sensitivity to written working
is therefore weak evidence that the working is used, and these 0.6B results need not
transfer to 1.7B.

\textbf{Hint test.} Prior work reports that hint use often goes
unmentioned~\cite{turpin2023,chen2025}; here every follower acknowledged the hint, so we
observed no undisclosed influence under this protocol. The larger effect was
disruption: the hint lengthened thinking and left six previously correct responses
unfinished. Responses that never reach an answer cannot be scored by the consistency
checker, so a hint can damage reasoning without producing any detectable
inconsistency. The hint run's different hardware (CPU, 32-bit floats,
\texttt{ATEN\_CPU\_CAPABILITY=default}, versus GPU, 16-bit) also means equal seeds need
not give equal samples.
