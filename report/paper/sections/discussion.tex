\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{Consistency is not faithfulness.} Whenever the checker could compare them,
Qwen3's final answer matched the answer its working arrived at: 0 of 45 thinking and
0 of 38 no-thinking responses (Table~\ref{tab:gsm8k}). This is a result about
consistency. A model could reach its answer some other way and still write working
that ends on the same number, and output-level tests of this kind measure
self-consistency rather than faithfulness to the model's inner
workings~\cite{parcalabescu2024}. Our 0/83 bounds how often the answer visibly
contradicts the working (at most 4.3\% at 95\% confidence). It cannot show that the
working caused the answer.

\textbf{Why the null result.} The test only fires when the working and the answer come
apart, and on GSM8K the written arithmetic is almost always right: 4 of 924 checked
steps were wrong, and the model corrected one of them itself. With so few natural
errors, an answer that ignores its working has little chance to show, so the test has
low power on easy problems. ID~11 shows the opposite case. Without thinking, the model
summed $204+160+330$ to 700 instead of 694 and answered 700: the answer followed the
faulty working exactly, so it was consistent and wrong.

\textbf{Sensitivity to the working.} The planted-working test comes from the other
direction. With a wrong working in the prompt, Qwen3-0.6B's first answer digit follows
the working with probability 0.94--0.99. Given no working, it gives the true answer's
first digit with probability 0.68--0.83 (Table~\ref{tab:planted}). The model is
therefore sensitive to written reasoning, but this test cannot separate \emph{using}
the working from \emph{copying} its last number. In the copy-vs-use control,
the copying digit is the global top token on all 30 wrong-working prompts
(mean probability 0.859; Table~\ref{tab:copyuse}). Correct-working prompts also
repeat the intermediate sum on all 30 cases, suggesting that the prefix format
encourages repetition rather than completion of the final operation. This limits
what the planted-working result can establish about faithfulness; it does not
prove an internal copying mechanism. Both tests use Qwen3-0.6B, not the 1.7B
model whose outputs we checked, and the control uses original weights whereas
the earlier tracing setup used a transcoder replacement model.

\textbf{Thinking versus no thinking.} Thinking mode solved six problems that
no-thinking mode missed and none the other way (exact McNemar $p = 0.031$). Both
modes had zero contradictions, so we find no difference in consistency, and we do not
claim that thinking-mode reasoning is more faithful.

\textbf{Checker errors look like model errors.} Our first arithmetic checker flagged 53
invalid steps on a 20-problem pilot, and only one of them was a real model error.
Later, a stale summary and an uncommitted parser fix changed our reported counts until
we regenerated every number from the raw outputs. A formal checker is only as reliable
as the extraction in front of it.
