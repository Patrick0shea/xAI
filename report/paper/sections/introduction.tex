\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Reasoning models write out their working before they answer. Chain-of-thought
prompting made step-by-step working standard for arithmetic and other multi-step
problems~\cite{wei2022}, and Qwen3 builds a thinking mode and a non-thinking mode
into the same model~\cite{qwen3}. It is tempting to read the working as an
explanation of how the answer was reached. But an explanation can be plausible to a
reader without being faithful to the process behind the prediction~\cite{jacovi2020},
and nothing in training a model to give correct answers requires the working that
precedes them to be an honest account.

Most tests of chain-of-thought faithfulness intervene. They truncate, corrupt or
paraphrase the reasoning and check whether the answer changes~\cite{lanham2023}, or
they add a biasing feature or hint to the prompt and check whether the reasoning
mentions it~\cite{turpin2023,chen2025}. We ask a simpler question of the model's own,
unedited output: \emph{does the final answer follow from the written reasoning?} For
arithmetic this can be checked formally and without gold labels. We extract each
calculation from the working, re-evaluate it with exact rational arithmetic, and
compare the final answer with the answer the working itself arrives at.

We apply this test to Qwen3-1.7B on 50 GSM8K problems~\cite{cobbe2021}, with thinking
mode on and off. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a label-free consistency checker for natural-language arithmetic working, and a
  Z3 logic checker~\cite{demoura2008} validated on FOLIO annotations~\cite{han2024folio};
\item a null result: no final answer contradicted its own working in 83 comparable
  responses (95\% upper bound 4.3\%), while thinking mode raised accuracy from 80\%
  to 92\%;
\item an analysis of what the null result can and cannot show. A matching answer is
  \emph{consistent} with the reasoning, not necessarily \emph{faithful} to
  it~\cite{parcalabescu2024}. We probe the gap with planted working and a
  copy-vs-use control.

\end{itemize}
